01

Getting started

Before you can use the app, you go through three screens: the Welcome screen, the Disclaimer, and the Setup page.

Welcome screen

Welcomes you to the app and explains the first workout is free with no subscription required.

Disclaimer

The disclaimer screen is titled “Before You Start.” The disclaimer has five sections: Health & Safety, Exercise Carries Risk, Limitation of Liability, No Medical Advice, and Your Responsibility.

Tapping I Understand records your acceptance, and it's stored off your device. If the disclaimer text changes in a future version, the screen shows again.

Setup page

Fill it in once, tap Save, and you land on the Home tab. That Setup page never appears again.

  • Name — optional.
  • Notes for your coach — optional. Background the AI Coach uses.
  • Goal — required. Build Strength, Build Muscle, or Both.
  • Experience — required. Beginner, Intermediate, or Advanced.
  • The 7-day question — required. “Have you lifted weights in the last 7 days?” Your answer sets whether your first week is a ramp-in week: if you have lifted in the last 7 days, your first week is not a ramp-in week; if you have not lifted in the last 7 days, your first week is a ramp-in week. The answer is only used once, if you have no workout history at all. See Ramp-in.

Save stays disabled until Goal, Experience, and the 7-day question are all picked. Name and Notes do not block Save.

When you tap Save, a program is chosen for you based on your Goal and Experience. That automatic picking only runs before you have visited either the Home tab or the Program tab. Once you have visited either one, your program stays put — changing Goal or Experience later does not silently replace it.

Setup is not Settings. After that first Save, the same information lives in the Settings tab. There is no Save button there — Name, Notes, Goal, and Experience save automatically as you change them, with a “Saved ✓” indicator. See Settings & profile.

03

Home

The main screen. Top to bottom: your workout counts, your Club Pass, your next workout, your program progress, your most recent personal record, your recent volume, and your last workouts.

Workout Counts

Three boxes at the top of the Home tab, counting the workouts you have finished.

The three boxes read This week, This Cycle — or This Phase on a Phases program — and Total. Each one is a count of finished workouts: how many you have done this week, how many in the current cycle or phase, and how many in all.

Club Pass

Your pass for gym check-in, opened from the Home tab.

Your Club Pass is on the Home tab. It is a QR code for gym check-in, and your name appears on it. Your name and Club Pass number are stored on the phone itself — see Settings & profile.

Next Workout

The card for your next workout.

The card is labeled Next Workout and shows the workout's title, how many exercises it has, and how long it runs. Start Workout begins the workout and takes you to the Exercise List. When a workout is in progress, this card shows the next workout in the program.

Current Program Progress

Where you are in your current program.

Current Program Progress shows your program's name, your current cycle and week — or your phase and week on a Phases program — with a ring showing your progress through the cycle. Every program runs in 12-week cycles — see Your program. Tap the card to open the Program tab.

Recent Personal Record

Your most recent personal record.

Recent Personal Record shows your most recent PR: the exercise, the weight, the reps, and the date you set it. Until you have logged one, it reads “No PR logged yet” — finish a workout and your heaviest set will show here.

Recent Volume

A bar chart of the volume in your most recent workouts.

Each bar is one of your recent workouts, and it shows that workout's total volume in pounds, written in thousands — for example, 12.4k lb. Normal workouts are red; a deload or ramp-in workout is yellow. The status labels appear on the Home tab as the week label while that week is current — see The four status labels in the Exercise List section for the full explanation. The chart appears once you have finished workouts to chart.

Last Workouts

Your most recent finished workouts.

Last workouts lists your four most recent finished workouts, newest first — each with its title, its date, and its total volume in pounds. Your full history is on the History tab.

04

Exercise List

When you start a workout, you land on the Exercise List — the in-session screen, which has no tabs. Each exercise for the session appears on its own card here.

Exercise cards

Each exercise in a workout has its own card, with Stats, Notes, and Modify buttons.

Stats

The Stats button opens a stats view for that exercise. The stats view shows how many sessions you have logged for the exercise, your best set, and your best estimated 1RM. Your best set is the heaviest weight at which you did all the recommended sets at the recommended reps. It is shown as weight, reps, and sets. Compound lifts also show a strength trend — your best weight per session — and every exercise shows a chart of your top set weight per session and a list of every session with the sets you logged.

Notes

The Notes button opens the exercise's Notes. The Notes show the Pro Tip form cues, the Target weight and reps, the Tempo, and any status explanation — for example a ramp-in, deload, plateau, or pain flag explanation when one applies to that exercise.

Tempo is shown in the Notes. The tempo is set per workout, so every exercise in the same workout uses the same tempo. Size workouts use “3-second eccentric, explosive concentric”. Strength workouts use “2-3 second eccentric, maximal-intent explosive concentric”. In the Size & Strength programs, the strength workouts use the strength tempo and the size workouts use the size tempo; the Phases programs use the size tempo. Full Body uses “2-second eccentric, 1-second concentric”.

Modify

The Modify button lets you swap the exercise in that slot for an alternative. Your pick becomes the slot's exercise going forward — for this workout and every workout after — until you change the pick in Modify again. See Swapping & supersets for how swaps and alternatives work.

Each exercise also has a photo, coaching cues, and a muscle diagram. On the diagram, a Primary / Secondary key is overlaid on the image, showing which muscles are primary and which are secondary for that exercise.

The four status labels

Ramp-in, deload, plateau, and pain flag — what each means, who assigns it, and where you see it.

Four conditions can attach to an exercise or a week. Each one is explained in text in the exercise's Notes, and each one shows as a label: ramp-in and deload appear as the week label on the Home tab while that week is current, and in History every label appears as a pill next to the workout's title on its History card. Ramp-in, deload, and plateau pills are yellow; the pain flag pill is red. History also shows a yellow ★ PR pill on any workout where a set beat that exercise's previous best.

How weights are picked

The target on each exercise card comes from that exercise's own history — all of it. If the exercise has no history, the target comes from another exercise that works the same muscle, and if there's no history for that muscle either, there's no target.

The weight pre-filled on an exercise card comes from your own logged history, if available. It is worked out per exercise from your own logged history, across every workout you have logged, not just workouts in your current program. After each finished workout, the app decides that exercise's next target:

  • Hit every planned set at the target reps → the weight goes up. The increase is a percentage of the weight you used: on compound lifts, 2.5% at 200 lb and above, 5% below that; on isolation lifts, 10% up to 20 lb, 7.5% up to 40 lb, and 5% above that. An increase is at least 5 lb, and every target is rounded to the nearest 2.5 lb.
  • Hit every set, but every set was RIR 0 → the weight holds. All-out grinders earn you another week at the same weight to own it, instead of an increase.
  • Miss the rep target → the weight holds. Same weight next time, until every planned set hits the target reps.
  • You lowered the weight yourself → that lower weight becomes the target. Whether or not you hit the reps at the decreased weight, the app keeps it for next time rather than jumping back up.
  • Deload weeks are skipped. A deload workout never changes the next normal week's target — the week after a deload picks up from the last normal week before it.
  • First time on an exercise. If you have never done that exercise, the target is estimated from your best exercise for the same muscle, at 90% — the card's note says “Target from your … work — first time on this lift, so adjust after the first set”.
  • Safety cap. Every prescribed weight is capped at 600 lb — and at 75 lb for dumbbell exercises. If an increase would pass the cap, the target holds at the cap.
  • History is the source of truth. Personal records, stall checks, and targets are all worked out from your full history. If a workout is deleted from History, nothing from it counts anymore — not toward targets, records, or anything else in the app.

Two things change the target for a single week without changing the progression itself: a deload (scheduled, or one you apply from the Coach tab) cuts the displayed weight 10% for that workout, and a ramp-in week takes a set away but leaves the weight alone.

Estimated 1RM does not set your weight

The estimated 1RM shown under the Target line is display only. It never feeds back into how your weights are prescribed. See Estimated 1RM.

Logging & RIR

How a set is saved, what RIR means, and what “Continue Workout” does and does not restore.

Logging a set

Tap Start on the exercise card, then when you finished the set, tap Rest to log it and start the rest timer. The same countdown shows on your Apple Watch. On the last set, the button reads Done, which logs the final set and opens the exercise summary.

A set is written and saved at the moment you log it — not at the end of the workout. After you log a set, the rest timer screen opens, where weight, reps, and RIR for that set can all be adjusted together; the set summary screen can adjust them too. You can watch the same countdown on your Apple Watch. Both screens save everything, including RIR.

If you leave mid-workout, Continue Workout on the Home tab brings the workout back with the sets you already logged intact.

RIR — Reps in Reserve

RIR is how many more reps you could have done when you finished the set. Finished with nothing left: RIR 0. Could have done two more: RIR 2. You log it per set, alongside weight and reps, by tapping 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, or 5+ on the rest timer or set summary screen. Tap the same number again to clear it. In a superset, each exercise gets its own RIR.

  • RIR is how Titanrep judges effort. The same weight at RIR 1 is a harder set than at RIR 3, and your history keeps the difference.
  • All-grinder rule: if you hit every planned set but every set was RIR 0, the weight holds next time instead of going up — see How weights are picked.
  • RIR shows in the History tab on any set that has a value.
  • Your average RIR is shown on the Coach tab in the “Stalled lifts” card, because a stall at low RIR means something different from a stall at high RIR — see Plateau. A stall is only flagged when the sets have RIR logged; without RIR values, the app cannot tell which kind of stall it is.

Watch app

Titanrep also runs on your Apple Watch. To install it, open the Watch app on your iPhone, find Titanrep under Available Apps, and tap Install. It installs automatically if Automatic App Install is on.

The watch app shows the rest timer. When a rest starts on the iPhone, the same countdown shows on your watch, with the set you're resting toward. The watch does not notify you when a rest starts — open the watch app yourself to see it. The watch only shows the timer; you log sets on the iPhone.

Swapping & supersets

Every slot has a default and at least three alternatives. Supersets keep both exercises' sets.

Alternatives for every slot

Every exercise that appears as a program default has at least 3 alternatives in its swap list. Tapping Modify on an exercise card opens the slot's alternatives — each alternate shows its own target weight, worked out from your history with that exercise. Your pick becomes the slot's exercise going forward — for this workout and every workout after — until you change the pick in Modify again, and picking the program default restores the default. If you swap after logging sets for the exercise in the slot, the logged sets are cleared, because the logged sets were a different movement. A pain flag is the one automatic change: a flagged default is substituted with an alternate on its own (see Pain flags). A modified pick is tied to the workout where you made it — delete that workout from History and the pick goes with it.

Supersets

In a superset, two exercises are paired and performed back to back. Both exercises are full exercises in their own right: each keeps its own sets, reps, weight, targets, and RIR when you log them, in History, and in an export. Exporting a superset exports the second exercise as its own entry — its sets are never dropped or folded into the first exercise.

Program resets

If you switch back to a program you have done before, Titanrep asks whether to pick up where you left off or start over at Week 1.

Ramp-in

Ramp-in is a week of less volume. The app assigns it when you haven't lifted in more than seven days.

A ramp-in is a gentler week — less volume than a normal week — for easing in, whether you're starting for the first time or coming back after time off.

The exact rule

  • More than 7 days off triggers it. Exactly 7 days does not. The gap is measured between your actual workouts.
  • It lasts one week.
  • During that week, every exercise has one fewer set (an exercise never drops below 1 set). The weight is not cut — the exercise's Notes say you can adjust the weight if necessary.
  • Ramp-in applies to every program. Ramp-in takes precedence over deload: if a week is both a scheduled deload week and a ramp-in week, it is a ramp-in week — and any ramp-in week clears the deload week in that same 6-week period.

If you have never logged a workout

With no history, there is no gap to measure, so the app uses your answer to the Setup page's 7-day question: if you said you had not worked out in the last 7 days, your first week is a ramp-in week. If you said you had, it is not. Once you have real history, the answer is never read again — which is also why it is not included in an export.

What you see

The RAMP-IN label on the exercise card and in History, with an explanation in the exercise's Notes. See The four status labels in the Exercise List section for the full explanation of all four labels.

Deload

A planned light week built into the program — not a reaction to a bad week.

A deload is a week of deliberately lighter work, scheduled into the program so recovery is planned instead of forced. In a deload week the app cuts the weight 10%, then sets sets and reps to reach 60% of the normal volume — a 40% volume cut — always keeping at least 2 sets. On bodyweight exercises, reps are cut to 60% instead. It is lighter by design, for that week only.

  • Which weeks: weeks 6 and 12 of every 12-week cycle. The exception is Full Body, whose only deload week is week 7.
  • A deload week does not set your weights back. Deload workouts are skipped when the app works out your next targets, so the week after a deload picks up from the last normal week before it.
  • A hard week, a missed week, or a week you choose to take easy does not become a scheduled deload.
  • A deload is not a ramp-in: deload comes from the calendar of the program, ramp-in comes from a gap in your attendance. But ramp-in takes precedence — any ramp-in week clears the deload week in that same 6-week period, so you never get a deload right after a ramp-in.

The one-time deload

Separately from the schedule, the Coach tab can offer you a one-time deload for a single muscle group when it detects a fatigue stall — the button reads “Deload [muscle] 10% next workout.” The button cuts that muscle group's weights 10% for the next workout, and tapping the button is also the action that records a plateau on the lift. It ends as soon as you complete a workout that trains that muscle group, or after 14 days, whichever comes first. See Plateau.

What you see

The DELOAD label on the exercise card and in History during that week, with an explanation in the exercise's Notes. See The four status labels in the Exercise List section for the full explanation of all four labels.

Coach

Tap Get AI Overview for an AI summary of your training — volume balance, progression, recovery, and what's next. Your stalled lifts, your current pain flags, and your benchmark lift charts are also on the Coach tab.

The AI Coach overview renders one sentence per line.

Program note

If your Goal and Experience in Settings point to a different program than the one you are using, the overview includes a highlighted “PROGRAM NOTE” box with this wording: “Your fitness goals in the Settings tab do not match your currently selected program. You can change your selected program in the Program tab.” There is no accept button, and the note never changes your program for you. The note appears when you generate or refresh the overview — changing Goal or Experience on its own does not show the note.

Estimated 1RM

An estimate of your one-rep max, worked out from sets you have already logged. Display only.

Your estimated 1RM is calculated from your best logged set on each lift with the Epley formula:

Estimated 1RM = weight × (1 + reps ÷ 30) — the Epley formula

The formula treats the logged reps as if they were done to failure, so the estimate reads low on any set you finished short of failure — a set finished at RIR 3 estimates lower than the same set taken to RIR 0.

Where it appears

  • In a workout, on the exercise card as an “Est. 1RM: X lb” line under the Target line; the same line appears on the workout preview page. The number is your best estimate across all your history for that exercise, rounded to a whole pound. The line only appears when that exercise has logged history; with no history there is nothing to estimate from.
  • On the Coach tab, in the Benchmark lifts card, as a trend chart per lift covering its last 12 sessions, so you can see the estimate move over time.

Plateau

The app detects the stall. Only you can apply the 10% drop — the app never cuts a weight on its own.

Titanrep watches for stalled lifts and shows them on the Coach tab in a “Stalled lifts” card. Detection is automatic; acting on it is not. Nothing changes until you tap the button.

How a stall is detected

  • Only compound lifts are checked, and only once you have at least 4 workouts logged for the lift.
  • A lift is flagged when its top weight has not gone up across its last 4 workouts — and only when those sets have RIR logged, because the kind of stall is judged from the average RIR:
  • Average RIR 1 or lower — a fatigue stall. The card says “You're grinding — fatigue is likely the cause,” and offers the deload button below.
  • Average RIR 3 or higher — an easy stall. The card says “Your sets felt easy — push harder.” There is no button; the fix is effort, not a weight cut.
  • Between those two, no alert is shown. The card shows the exercise name, how many workouts it has stalled for, its top weight, and its average RIR.

Applying the 10% drop

  1. On a fatigue stall, tap “Deload [muscle] 10% next workout.” That records the plateau on the lift — with its best set read from your workout history — and cuts that muscle group's weights 10% for the next workout.
  2. While the plateau is active, the card shows Remove Plateau instead. Tap Remove Plateau at any time to end the plateau yourself. Removing the plateau does not cancel the 10% deload if the deload has not been used yet — the deload still applies to the next workout for that muscle group.
  3. The 10% cut itself lasts one workout: it ends when you complete a workout that trains that muscle group, or after 14 days if you don't.

How it ends on its own

  • It clears automatically when the lift improves: when you finish a workout whose best set on that lift beats the best recorded before it — more weight, or the same weight with more reps — the plateau is removed.
  • An active plateau belongs to your current program and cycle: it is listed while you are on that program and cycle, and a new cycle or a different program leaves it behind.

Notes text for a plateaued exercise: “Plateau: this lift is stalled (best set X lb × Y reps). Weight cut 10% for this workout. Lasts until the lift improves, or until you remove it in the Coach tab.”

What you see

The PLATEAU label on the exercise card and in History while the plateau is selected. See The four status labels in the Exercise List section for the full explanation of all four labels.

Pain flags

Flag an exercise that bothered you, and it quietly stops being the default for its slot.

If an exercise bothers you — a knee, a shoulder, anything — flag it during the workout with the “Mark With Pain Flag” button. While an exercise is flagged, that button reads “Bothering me — tap to clear.” The flag stays on until you remove it — with that button, or with Clear flag next to the exercise in the Coach tab's “Exercises that bothered you” card. The flag does not expire on its own.

What a flag does

  • A flagged exercise is never the program default for its slot. An alternate is substituted in its place, silently — the workout just shows the alternate.
  • The one exception: if every exercise available for a slot is flagged, a flagged exercise is used anyway, so the slot is never empty.
  • The flagged exercise stays available as an alternate. Flagging removes it as the default, not from the app.
  • Your current flags are listed on the Coach tab, in the “Exercises that bothered you” card — exercise name, the date you flagged it, and any note. The AI Coach sees your flags in its overview. That current list is what transfers in an export.
  • Separately, each finished workout records which of its exercises were flagged at the time — that is the ⚠ PF pill on its History card. Those per-workout records stay with the workout; they are not part of the export file.

Notes text for a flagged exercise: “Pain flag: this exercise is flagged for pain. Stays flagged until you remove it.”

What you see

The red ⚠ PF label on the exercise card and in History. See The four status labels in the Exercise List section for the full explanation of all four labels.

History

The History tab is the source of truth for the whole app. Delete a workout there, and it is gone everywhere.

Your workout history. Every finished workout and its information. Expand the workout card to see the details. Each workout card shows the workout's title, program, date, duration, and volume, and carries its labels as pills next to the workout's title. Expand a workout card to see the workout's exercises and sets and the RIR values you logged; the ⚠ PF, ★ PR, and PLATEAU pills also appear next to each exercise name there. The pills appear in this order: ⚠ PF in red first, then ★ PR, then PLATEAU, then DELOAD or RAMP-IN in yellow. The labels appear on History cards as pills — see The four status labels in the Exercise List section for the full explanation of what each label means. Each card has a Delete button — tap Delete, then Tap again to delete, to remove the workout.

Why “source of truth” matters

  • Target weights, personal records, plateau bests, and estimated 1RM are all worked out from History, not from separate stored numbers.
  • Deleting a workout removes everything that came from it: its sets, any Modify picks made in it, and its effect on targets, records, and plateau detection. A deleted test workout leaves no trace anywhere in the app.
  • Because of this, an import that brings your history over brings your targets and records with it — they are rebuilt from the history itself.

Settings

Your name, Notes for your coach, Goal, and Experience — all saved automatically — plus the Export Workout Data and Import Workout Data buttons and the Disclaimer card.

Export & Import

Moving to a new phone: exactly what is in the JSON file, and exactly what is not.

Export writes your data to a single JSON file; Import reads that file on the new phone and restores the data. The buttons are in the Settings tab, under “Your Workout Data.” Export only shows when you have workouts to export. Both buttons are a Premium feature — see Premium & the paywall, below.

✓ In the file

  • All finished workouts — every exercise, set, rep, weight, target, and RIR value
  • Supersets in full — both exercises, each as its own entry with its own sets
  • Your profile — current program, Goal, Experience, days per week, equipment, and your Notes for your coach (the 7-day answer is not included — see below)
  • Current pain flags — the flags list from the Coach tab's “Exercises that bothered you” card
  • Program resets — the start-over markers, matched by workout date, so week counting carries over

✕ Not in the file

  • In-progress workouts — only finished workouts export; a workout in progress on the old phone is lost
  • The 7-day answer — it is only read when there is no history at all, and an import always brings history, so it would never be used
  • Your name and Club Pass number — both are stored on the phone itself, not in the profile, and they are not included
  • Apple Health data
  • Modify picks — the swaps you chose inside individual workouts
  • Your stored target weights as numbers — targets are not carried over as figures; they are rebuilt from the imported history by the same rules that built them originally
  • Per-workout pain-flag badges — the History badges do not transfer; the current flags list does

How an import lands

  • Importing adds to what is on the phone — it does not wipe anything first. A workout whose date is already on the phone is skipped, and the result message tells you how many workouts and sets were imported, how many were skipped, and names any exercises in the file that this phone's app does not have.
  • The imported profile overwrites the profile on the phone, and the imported pain flags replace the phone's current flag list.
  • Program resets are matched to workouts by date, so they only land if the workout they follow was imported too (or was already there).

Who can use it

Export and Import are locked to paying users. If you are not paying, the buttons are still shown in Settings, but disabled, with the note “Export and Import are a Premium feature.” — so you can see what you are missing. Tapping a disabled button does nothing; there is no paywall popup from these buttons.

Records stored on Google Firebase servers

The app stores two kinds of records on Google Firebase servers:

  • A disclaimer acceptance record, written when you tap I Understand: anonymous customer ID, disclaimer version, time of acceptance, app version, device model and iOS version; kept 7 years.
  • A premium usage log, written each time the app lets you into a premium feature (a workout past the free one, Export, Import): anonymous customer ID, time, which premium feature was used, and — when available — the App Store transaction ID for your current subscription; kept 1 year.

Neither record contains a name, email address, or workout data.

The upgrade-only rule

  • You can only move up, never down. Monthly can upgrade to Annual or Lifetime. Annual can upgrade to Lifetime. Lifetime buys nothing else — there is nothing above it.
  • There is no “Best Value” badge on Annual, because Annual is not the best value — Lifetime is.

  • The rule itself is printed on the paywall, centered below Restore Purchases: “Subscription downgrades are not allowed until the current subscription expires. Upgrades take effect immediately.” Plans you cannot buy are simply disabled.

The rating prompt

Titanrep only asks for an App Store rating in a narrow case: right after a paying user — not on the free trial — finishes a workout in which they hit a personal record, and never after the first workout. Trial and free users are never asked.

Settings & profile

No Save button — everything here saves itself, and says so.

Every change on the Settings tab shows a green “Saved ✓” indicator beside the heading of the section just changed — Your Name, Notes for your coach, Your Goal, or Experience. The indicator disappears after about two seconds. If you do not see the indicator, the change has not saved.

Your Name appears on your Club Pass. The name is stored on the phone itself, and the name saves when you leave the field.

Notes for your coach are the background the AI Coach reads when the AI Coach advises you. Edits save automatically when you leave the field. The notes are part of your profile, so the notes are included in an export.

Goal and Experience save automatically too. Changing Goal or Experience after setup does not change your program by itself (see Your program); if Goal and Experience no longer match your program, the Coach tab says so.

The Export Workout Data and Import Workout Data buttons live under “Your Workout Data.” Export appears only when you have workouts. For non-paying users, both buttons are shown disabled with the note “Export and Import are a Premium feature.”

The Disclaimer card — headed “Health & Safety” over the title “Disclaimer” — starts collapsed. Tap the card's chevron to read the full disclaimer text you accepted before your first workout.

The app version appears at the bottom of the Settings tab.

Two things that live outside the export

Your name and Club Pass number are stored on the phone itself, not in the workout database, and they are not part of the export file. On a new phone, enter them again. Your Club Pass itself — a QR code for gym check-in — is opened from the Home tab.

Questions & answers

The not-obvious things, asked the way people actually ask them.

Why does my workout have fewer sets this week?

Almost always a ramp-in: you were away more than 7 days, so every exercise loses one set for one week. Check for the RAMP-IN label. Exactly 7 days away does not trigger it — it has to be more than 7.

Why is this exercise suddenly a different exercise?

If the exercise has a pain flag, it is never the slot's default — an alternate is substituted silently. Remove the flag from the Coach tab's “Exercises that bothered you” card and it can be the default again.

Why did my weight drop 10%?

Two things do that, and both are temporary. It is a deload week (week 6 or 12 of your cycle; week 7 on Full Body), where the 10% cut comes with a 40% volume cut — or it is the one-time deload you applied from a “Stalled lifts” card in the Coach tab, which cuts one muscle group 10% for one workout. The Phases programs have no scheduled deload weeks, so on a Phases program a 10% drop is the one-time deload. Nothing in Titanrep cuts a weight without one of those two reasons, and neither one changes your underlying progression.

What is the “Stalled lifts” card on the Coach tab?

The app flagging a compound lift whose top weight has not gone up in its last 4 workouts. If your average RIR on it is 1 or lower, it is treated as a fatigue stall and you can apply a one-time 10% deload for that muscle group. If your average RIR is 3 or higher, the card just tells you the sets felt easy — push harder. See Plateau.

The app says my goals don't match my program. What do I do?

Nothing, unless you want to. It is a suggestion in the highlighted “PROGRAM NOTE” box that appears with the AI overview on the Coach tab, with no accept button. To act on it, change your selected program in the Program tab yourself.

I deleted a test workout. Why did my targets change?

Because History is the source of truth. Targets, records, and plateau bests are all worked out from History, so deleting the workout removes its effect everywhere.

I got a new phone. What will I lose?

Export on the old phone and import on the new one, and you keep your finished workouts, your profile (including your Notes for your coach), your current pain flags, and your program resets. You lose: any workout in progress at export time, your Modify picks, your name and Club Pass number (re-enter them), and the 7-day answer (never needed once history exists). Export and Import require Premium.

Why is there no Est. 1RM on this exercise?

It only appears when the exercise has logged history. Log a set for it and the estimate appears under the Target line.

Can I downgrade from Annual to Monthly?

No. The paywall is upgrades only: Monthly → Annual or Lifetime, Annual → Lifetime. A plan you cannot buy explains why its button is disabled.

Where are Export and Import?

In the Settings tab, under “Your Workout Data.”
